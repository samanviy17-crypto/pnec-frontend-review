Resources · Fire Safety

Wildfire Defensible Space: Trees Near Your Home

Start close to your home. Plan new trees for their mature size, maintain existing vegetation, and check which rules apply to your property.

Sources checked November 17, 2026. Educational guidance, not a parcel inspection or emergency alert.

Which rules apply?

Local requirements: Poway Municipal Code §15.05.050 separates new landscaping from ongoing vegetation management. Its §603.2 covers new planting in SRA and LRA land designated Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone and/or WUI. The §603.4.2 tree standards concern the 30-foot area around a structure. Existing-building maintenance has its own scope in §604.2. Read Poway’s current codified requirements (Ord. 878, 2025). The signed ordinance, page 41, records adoption December 2, 2025 and specifies that it takes effect 30 days after adoption. Its preamble identifies January 1, 2026 for the 2025 state code edition.

State requirements: PRC §4291 addresses covered buildings on or adjoining specified wildland in the State Responsibility Area; Government Code §51182 addresses covered occupied structures in Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones in the Local Responsibility Area. Property boundaries, exceptions and applicable local rules matter. PRC §4291 · Gov. Code §51182.

Confirm your parcel’s designation, approved landscape plan and any protected vegetation with Poway’s property/records resources and Fire Prevention before removing trees or treating a diagram as a compliance finding. For a project governed by an earlier permit or approved landscape plan, confirm the applicable code edition and compliance date with Poway Fire Prevention.

Three zones, measured outward

Measure from the structure and applicable attachments, not the trunk. Poway’s Home Ignition Zones explains the closest-to-home priority.

Zone 0 · 0–5 feet

Reduce ember ignition

Use noncombustible surfaces and keep combustible vegetation, mulch and debris away from the building and attachments. Poway §603.5 specifies this for applicable fuel-modification landscaping; do not assume the pending statewide rule has the same commencement date.

Zone 1 · 5–30 feet

Maintain a low-fuel area

Remove dead growth and separate vegetation. For applicable Poway landscaping, §603.5 calls for irrigated fire-smart planting to the property line if closer.

Zone 2 · 30–100 feet

Reduce available fuel

Maintain vegetation and spacing, subject to the property boundary and local requirements. Avoid wholesale clearing: Poway has specific limits on reducing native vegetation and removing protected growth.

New trees and existing trees are different

New planting · mature drip line
For new trees subject to Poway §603.4.2, the mature canopy’s drip line must remain at least 10 feet from a combustible structure.
New planting · canopy separation
That section requires at least 20 feet of horizontal clearance between a new tree canopy and adjacent tree canopies. It is not a universal 20-foot rule for every existing tree.
Existing trees · chimney outlets
Poway §603.4.2 specifies at least 10 feet between existing branches and chimney/stovepipe outlets. This is not measured from the trunk or automatically from every roof edge.
Existing roofs · recommended clearance
Poway’s existing-home best practices recommend keeping branches at least 5 feet above the roof, away from eaves, and 10 feet from chimney/stovepipe outlets. The roof recommendation is not presented here as a universal new-tree planting law.
Roof and vegetation maintenance
Remove dead vegetation and roof/gutter debris. CAL FIRE recommends slope-dependent canopy gaps: 10, 20 or 30 feet for increasing slope bands. Do not substitute those general guidance values for a stricter applicable local new-tree rule.

Source: Poway §15.05.050, modified CWUIC §§603.2, 603.4.2, 603.5 and 604.2–604.3.3. Check approvals before removing heritage trees or vegetation in stream/habitat areas; native vegetation clearing can require a permit.
